What It Really Costs to Build a Custom Home in Edmonton.

An honest, plain-language breakdown of every component that shapes your budget — from the lot beneath you to the finishes you'll live with.

O
Orchid Team Updated June 2026 · 6 min read

One of the first questions every Edmonton family or investor asks us is simple: what will this actually cost? It's also the hardest question to answer in a single number, because a custom home is exactly that — custom. The honest answer depends on your lot, your design, your finishing choices, and the surprises hiding underground. What we can do is walk you through every component that goes into the price, so you can build a realistic budget before you fall in love with a floor plan.

Start with the land, not the house

In Edmonton, the cost conversation begins with the lot. A mature infill lot in a tree-lined neighbourhood like Glenora or Westmount carries a very different price than a parcel in a newer suburb, and that difference ripples through the entire project. Infill sites often need demolition of an existing structure, utility disconnects, and careful grading — costs that don't exist on a clean suburban lot. Where you build also affects what you're allowed to build, which shapes the design budget before a single drawing is finished.

The major cost categories

Beyond the land, the build itself breaks down into a handful of categories. Design and architectural planning come first, followed by engineering — structural, geotechnical, and grading — which is non-negotiable and protects you from the most expensive kind of mistake. Permitting with the City of Edmonton is a smaller line item but a critical one for your timeline. Then comes the bulk of the budget: the foundation, framing, and the licensed trades for electrical, gas, plumbing, HVAC, and mechanical work. Finally, interior finishing and exterior landscaping carry an enormous range, because this is where your personal taste meets your wallet.

  • Design & architectural planning — concept through construction-ready drawings
  • Engineering — structural, geotechnical and grading (non-negotiable)
  • City of Edmonton permitting — small line item, big timeline impact
  • Structure & trades — foundation, framing, electrical, gas, plumbing, HVAC, mechanical
  • Finishing & landscaping — the widest range, driven by your selections

Why "cost per square foot" can mislead you

You'll see a lot of per-square-foot figures online, especially in local forums, and they're a useful starting point — but they hide as much as they reveal. Two homes of identical size can differ dramatically once you account for finishing level, site complexity, and whether the build is a single detached home or a duplex. A walk-out basement, a complex roofline, or premium millwork can move the number significantly. Treat per-square-foot estimates as a rough range to begin the conversation, not a quote.

The seams between contractors — not any single line item — are where most budgets quietly blow up.

How the all-under-one-roof model affects your budget

A big reason budgets blow up isn't any single line item — it's the seams between contractors. When the architect, the permit office, the demolition crew, and the finishing trades all operate independently, delays and miscommunication turn into change orders, and change orders turn into cost. Because Orchid manages design, permits, engineering, trades, and construction as one accountable team, the budget you agree to at the start stays connected to the budget you live with at the end. Fewer handoffs means fewer surprises.

Building a budget you can trust

The best way to avoid a nasty surprise is to plan the whole project — lot, drawings, permits, and engineering — before construction begins. Most Edmonton delays and overruns trace back to permitting or soil conditions, and both are predictable when you investigate them up front. We'll map your project from lot to possession and give you numbers you can actually plan around.
