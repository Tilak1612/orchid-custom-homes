Choosing a builder: 10 questions to ask in Edmonton.

The right questions reveal how a builder actually operates — long before the first shovel hits the ground. Here are ten that separate the pros from the rest.

O
Orchid Team Updated May 2026 · 8 min read

Choosing a builder is the single biggest decision in a custom home project — bigger than the lot, bigger than the floor plan. The right partner makes the process feel calm and predictable; the wrong one turns it into eighteen months of surprises. The hard part is that almost every builder sounds great in a first meeting. What separates them is how they answer specific, slightly uncomfortable questions about money, timelines and accountability.

Below are ten questions we encourage every Edmonton homeowner to ask — including of us. A confident builder will answer all of them clearly and in writing.

Credentials, licensing and warranty

Start with the basics that protect you legally. In Alberta, new homes are generally required to be covered by a recognized new-home warranty, and builders need to be licensed accordingly. Ask directly:

  • Are you licensed to build new homes in Alberta, and will this home be registered for new-home warranty before construction begins?
  • Who is the warranty provider, and what is covered for how long?
  • Do you carry liability insurance and workers' coverage for everyone on site?

If a builder is vague about warranty registration, treat it as a red flag and verify current requirements with the appropriate Alberta authority yourself.

Pricing structure and allowances

Ask whether the contract is fixed-price or cost-plus, and how allowances are set. Allowances — the budgeted amounts for items like flooring, fixtures and cabinetry — are where quotes quietly diverge. A low headline price often hides thin allowances you will blow past at the selection stage.

Ask to see the allowance schedule line by line. Two quotes that look identical at the top can differ by tens of thousands once you choose real finishes.

Change-order and overage policy

Changes happen on every build. What matters is the process. Ask how change orders are priced, approved and documented, and what happens if a trade or material cost rises mid-project. A clear, written change-order policy is one of the strongest signals of an organized builder.

Timeline, communication and the team

Find out who you will actually deal with day to day, how often you will get updates, and what a realistic timeline looks like for your build — including Edmonton's winter and permitting realities. Ask:

  • Who is my single point of contact, and how do I reach them?
  • What is your realistic schedule, and how do you handle delays?
  • How many projects do you run at once?

References and finished work

Ask to speak with recent clients — ideally one whose home is a year or two old, so you hear how the builder handled warranty and follow-up, not just the honeymoon period. Visit a current site if you can: a tidy, well-run job site tells you a great deal about how your project will be managed.

Local experience in Edmonton

Building infill in a mature neighbourhood is a different discipline from a greenfield suburb. Ask about the builder's experience with your specific context — tight lots, neighbour relations, lot servicing, the consolidated zoning bylaw, and City of Edmonton permitting timelines. Local fluency saves months.
