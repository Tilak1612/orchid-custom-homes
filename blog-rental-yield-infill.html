Maximizing rental yield on an infill duplex.

Suite layouts, legal secondary suites and the right finish choices can lift income meaningfully — without overbuilding for the neighbourhood you are betting on.

O
Orchid Team Updated May 2026 · 9 min read

An infill duplex in a mature Edmonton neighbourhood can be one of the most efficient ways to build long-term wealth from a single lot. But the difference between a duplex that comfortably carries itself and one that merely breaks even usually comes down to decisions made on paper, long before construction — how many legal doors you create, how each suite is laid out, and where you spend (and don't spend) on finishes. This guide covers the levers that move yield, with every number framed as illustrative. Real returns depend on your lot, costs and the rental market at the time you lease.

More legal doors, more income

Yield scales with the number of legal, rentable units. A side-by-side duplex gives you two doors; add a legal basement suite under each side and a single lot can carry up to four households. Recent zoning changes in Edmonton have made multi-unit small-scale residential far more achievable on standard lots — which is exactly why infill has become so attractive to local investors.

  • Two-door duplex — simplest to build and manage, strong appreciation on the land.
  • Duplex + secondary suites — up to four income streams, higher yield, more construction and management complexity.

Each additional legal suite must meet building and fire-separation requirements, have compliant egress, and often its own entrance and parking. Confirm what your lot and the current bylaw allow before you bank on a fourth door.

Layout choices that rent well

Tenants pay for usable space and privacy, not raw square footage. A well-planned 800 sq ft suite with two real bedrooms and good light will out-rent a larger but awkward one. Prioritise:

  • Separate, private entrances for every suite — tenants value not sharing a door.
  • Two bedrooms where the budget allows; they broaden the tenant pool and lift rent.
  • In-suite laundry, which is consistently one of the highest-value amenities renters cite.
  • Generous natural light in basement suites — larger windows and proper egress wells pay for themselves.
Build for the tenant you actually want for the long term. A slightly better layout and in-suite laundry can mean lower vacancy and fewer turnovers — which protects yield more than a higher headline rent ever will.

Finishes: durable beats luxurious

In a rental, the smart spend is on durability and low maintenance, not premium aesthetics. Luxury vinyl plank flooring, quartz or quartz-look counters, quality hardware and easy-clean surfaces look great, survive turnovers, and keep maintenance calls down. Over-finishing a rental rarely returns its cost in rent; under-building with cheap materials costs you in repairs and vacancy. Aim for the durable middle.

Don't overbuild for the neighbourhood

One of the most common infill mistakes is building a suite that is too expensive for the area's rent ceiling. Every neighbourhood has a realistic rent range; spending to a finish level the local market won't pay for simply lowers your yield. Research comparable rents in the specific area before you finalise the spec, and match your investment to what tenants there will actually pay.

Running the numbers honestly

A credible yield estimate accounts for vacancy, property management, maintenance reserves, insurance, utilities you cover, and financing costs — not just gross rent. Build a conservative model, stress-test it against higher vacancy and interest rates, and treat any projected return as illustrative until you have real leases in hand. Disciplined underwriting is what separates a sound infill investment from an optimistic one.
