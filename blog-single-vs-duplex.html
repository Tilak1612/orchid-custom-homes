Single detached vs duplex: which should you build?

On a typical Edmonton lot the choice comes down to buildable area, rental potential and resale — and the zoning bylaw quietly steers the whole decision.

O
Orchid Team Updated May 2026 · 9 min read

You own — or are about to buy — a lot in a mature Edmonton neighbourhood, and you can already picture two very different futures on it. One is a single detached custom home: spacious, private, the kind of house a family settles into for decades. The other is a side-by-side duplex: two homes on one parcel, more density, and the option to live in one half while the other pays part of your mortgage. Which is the right call?

There is no universal answer. The best choice depends on your lot, your goals, your appetite for being a landlord, and what the current zoning permits. This guide walks through the trade-offs the way we do with clients at the kitchen table.

Start with zoning, not floor plans

Before you fall in love with a design, find out what your lot actually allows. Edmonton's consolidated zoning bylaw expanded what is permitted in many residential districts, and lots that once allowed only a single home may now support a duplex or more units. Lot width, depth, corner status and overlays all influence the outcome. The zoning, not the wish list, sets the outer boundary of what you can build.

The most expensive mistake we see is designing first and checking zoning second. Confirm permitted uses and site rules up front, and the rest of the decision gets far simpler.

Buildable area and how the lot is used

A single detached home typically gives you more contiguous interior space, a larger private yard, and simpler massing. A duplex splits the same footprint into two narrower homes, often two or three storeys to recover the floor area lost to the party wall and side setbacks. On a standard 50-foot mature lot, a duplex usually means two roughly 1,400–1,800 sq ft homes (illustrative), each with its own entrance and often a basement that can be developed as a legal suite.

  • Single detached — more space per household, bigger yard, broadest resale buyer pool.
  • Duplex — more total sellable or rentable floor area, two income or sale events, denser use of the land.

Income potential vs resale simplicity

A duplex opens income strategies a single home cannot match: sell both halves, rent both, or live in one and rent the other. Add legal basement suites and a single lot can carry up to four households. That income can offset carrying costs meaningfully — though the figures vary widely by neighbourhood and finish level, so treat any projection as illustrative and run your own numbers.

A single detached home is simpler to own, finance and eventually sell. It appeals to the largest pool of buyers and carries no tenant-management overhead. If your priority is a forever home rather than a return, simplicity has real value.

Cost and complexity

A duplex costs more to build in absolute terms — two kitchens, two mechanical systems, fire-separation between units, sometimes separate services — but often less per square foot than a single home of the same total area. It also involves more permitting and, if you are creating separate titles, a subdivision process. Budget for the extra design, engineering and approval time a two-unit build requires.

Who each option suits

Choose a single detached home if you want maximum space and privacy, the simplest path to possession, and the broadest resale appeal. Choose a duplex if you want to make the land work harder — building equity through rental income, house-hacking your own mortgage, or creating two sale events from one purchase. Many of our Edmonton infill clients land on the duplex precisely because mature-lot land is the scarce, appreciating asset, and density is how you capture its value.

How Orchid helps

We start every project with a feasibility look at your specific lot — zoning, buildable area, and a realistic comparison of single versus duplex outcomes — before any design work begins. That way the decision is grounded in what the land can actually deliver, not a guess.
