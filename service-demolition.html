Overview

Demolition done right, before the build begins.

For most infill projects, demolition is where the timeline is quietly won or lost. An aging house has to come down safely, hazardous materials have to be removed and documented, utilities have to be properly disconnected, and the lot has to be cleared and graded so the next crew can start without surprises. We treat all of it as the opening phase of your build, not a separate contract you have to chase.

In Edmonton's mature neighbourhoods, that means working carefully alongside occupied homes, protecting shared property lines, and meeting City requirements for demolition permits, asbestos surveys and waste handling. Coordinating that in-house keeps the site safe and the neighbours onside — and keeps your start date intact.

Because the same team carries the project from teardown through engineering and construction, nothing is dropped at the hand-off. The crew that clears your lot already knows what's going up next, so the site is prepped exactly the way the foundation needs it.

Frequently Asked

Demolition questions, answered.

What Edmonton infill owners most often want to know before the teardown begins.

Do I need a permit to demolish a house in Edmonton?
Yes. The City of Edmonton requires a demolition permit, and a hazardous-material survey is typically needed before that permit is issued. We handle the survey, the permit application and the utility disconnect coordination as part of the job, so you're not navigating the City process or the abatement requirements on your own.
How do you protect the houses next door on a tight infill lot?
Mature Edmonton lots often sit close to their neighbours, so we plan the teardown around those constraints — sequencing the demolition to keep equipment clear of property lines, managing dust and debris, and protecting shared fences and landscaping where we can. Because the same team carries the project into engineering and construction, we already know how the new foundation needs the site left.
What happens to the debris and old materials?
Hazardous materials are removed and disposed of by licensed abatement trades with documentation. The remaining debris is hauled away and, wherever practical, recyclable material like concrete, metal and clean wood is diverted from landfill rather than dumped. We manage the bins and disposal so the lot ends the process clean and ready for the next phase.
