Overview

Engineered before it's ever framed.

Every Orchid home rests on a chain of engineering decisions made long before the first wall goes up. We coordinate the structural design that carries your roof and floor loads, the geotechnical work that tells us what your soil can actually hold, and the lot grading that keeps water moving away from your foundation — all reviewed and stamped by licensed engineers.

Edmonton's clay-heavy soils, frost depth and infill setbacks make this work matter more here than in many markets. A two-storey added over a mature lot, a basement suite, or a wide-span great room each changes the loads and the soil response — and we'd rather catch that on paper than at footing inspection.

Because we manage engineering as part of one continuous build, the structural drawings, the geotech report and the architectural plans all speak the same language. There's no gap where one consultant's assumptions quietly contradict another's — the seams are ours to close, not yours.

Frequently Asked

Engineering questions, answered.

What Edmonton homeowners and infill investors most often ask about the engineering on their build.

Why does Edmonton soil need its own engineering attention?
Much of the Edmonton region sits on clay and till that can swell, shrink and shift with moisture, and our frost depth means footings have to reach well below grade. A geotechnical investigation tells us exactly what your lot will bear, so the foundation is sized for your soil rather than a generic assumption — that's what prevents settlement cracks and costly remediation years down the road.
Do you provide the engineer, or do I hire my own?
We coordinate engineering for you as part of the build. The structural, geotechnical and grading work is performed and stamped by licensed professional engineers we work with, and we manage the scheduling, the hand-offs to our architectural and trade drawings, and the City submission — so you have one accountable team rather than a folder of disconnected consultants.
Does an infill build need more engineering than a new-community home?
Often, yes. Mature Edmonton lots come with tight setbacks, neighbouring foundations to protect, aged servicing and grading that has to tie into existing streetscapes. We plan the structural and grading engineering around those constraints early, so the design that gets permitted is one that can actually be built on your specific lot.
