Overview

Budget clarity is part of the build.

The financial side of building a home is where good projects most often get stressful — not because the numbers are bad, but because they're unclear. Orchid brings the budget, the financing process and the draw schedule into the open from day one, so you always know what's been spent, what's coming, and how it lines up with your lender.

We're a builder, not a lender. The financing terms always come from your chosen bank or institution. What we provide is introductions to lenders experienced with construction financing, a clear explanation of how draws work, and hands-on management of the draw schedule against real build milestones — so funds are requested at the right stage and your lender stays comfortable throughout.

For investors building infill, that discipline matters even more. Keeping draws, milestones and the budget tightly coordinated is what keeps a project's numbers predictable from teardown to handover, instead of drifting as the build progresses.

Frequently Asked

Funding questions, answered.

What Edmonton homeowners and infill investors most often ask about financing and budgeting a build.

Does Orchid lend the money or provide the financing?
No — we're a builder, not a lender. All financing and the terms that come with it are provided by your chosen bank or institution. What we do is introduce you to lenders experienced with construction financing, explain clearly how the process and draws work, and manage the draw schedule against your build milestones so the financing side stays organized and on track.
How does a construction draw schedule actually work?
Construction financing typically releases money in stages, or "draws," as the build reaches defined milestones — foundation, framing, lock-up, completion and so on — rather than all at once. We coordinate those draw requests against real progress on site, so the funds release at the right time and your lender can confirm the work supports each advance. It keeps your cash flow and the build in step.
How do you keep an Edmonton build from going over budget?
Most budget overruns come from vague allowances and decisions left until mid-build. We set a clear, line-item budget up front with realistic allowances and contingency, then track spend against it as the project moves. Combined with managing every trade in-house, that visibility means selections and changes are priced before they're committed — so the final number reflects the plan rather than a string of surprises.
