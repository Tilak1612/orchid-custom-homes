Overview

One team, teardown to topping out.

Infill is unforgiving of seams. A teardown that runs late delays the permit; a permit hold-up idles the engineer; an engineering surprise stalls the framer — and on a tight mature lot, every handoff is a place the schedule can break. Orchid removes those seams by owning every stage of the development in-house.

We start by reading the lot's development potential — what the zoning permits, how the site drains, what the neighbours and the bylaw will support — then carry it through permitted demolition, abatement and utility disconnects, into stamped engineering and full construction. For investors that means a predictable timeline and a budget that holds; for homeowners, it means one accountable contact instead of a chain of contractors pointing at each other.

We develop infill across Edmonton's established communities and the surrounding region — St. Albert, Sherwood Park, Spruce Grove, Leduc and Beaumont — wherever the zoning supports it.

Frequently Asked

Infill development questions.

What Edmonton investors and homeowners ask before starting an infill project with Orchid.

What infill formats can I build in Edmonton?
It depends entirely on the lot's zoning. Edmonton's mature neighbourhoods support a range of formats — single detached, skinny homes, side-by-side duplexes and, on the right lots, multi-family. We assess the specific parcel's zoning and dimensions up front and tell you exactly what's permitted, so you design to the maximum the bylaw allows rather than discovering limits late.
Do you handle the demolition of the existing house?
Yes — permitted demolition is part of our infill service. We manage abatement, utility disconnects, the teardown itself and haul-away, all properly permitted and inspection-ready. Because the same team then handles engineering and construction, there's no gap or finger-pointing between the demolition crew and the builder — it's one continuous, accountable process.
I'm an investor — how do you keep the numbers predictable?
We assess development potential and price the full scope before you commit, so the yield math is grounded in real costs rather than estimates that drift. From there, the one-team model is what protects the budget: demolition, permits, engineering and construction all sit under one project manager, which means fewer change orders, tighter sequencing and a timeline you can plan a sale or lease-up around.
