Overview

Curb appeal that's part of the build.

On too many projects, the home is finished and the yard is an afterthought — a muddy lot the owner is left to sort out long after they've moved in. Orchid treats exterior finishing as the closing phase of the build itself. Final grading, driveways, walkways, decks, fences and planting are scoped, budgeted and scheduled alongside everything else, so the property is complete on possession day.

It also matters more than looks. Final grading has to carry surface water away from your foundation, driveways and walkways have to be built for Edmonton's freeze-thaw cycle, and planting has to suit our climate to actually survive the first winter. Done as part of the build, the grading ties cleanly into the engineered drainage we already designed for the lot.

Because the same crew that built the home finishes the grounds, there's no gap between trades and no second contractor to coordinate. The exterior is held to the same standard as the interior — and signed off in the same final walkthrough.

Frequently Asked

Exterior questions, answered.

What Edmonton homeowners most often ask about finishing the grounds around a new build.

Is landscaping included, or is it a separate job after I move in?
We scope exterior finishing as part of the build so it's planned, budgeted and scheduled with the rest of the project. That means final grading, driveways, decks and planting can be completed by possession rather than left as a muddy lot you have to organize afterward. The exact scope is agreed during design, so there are no surprises about what's included.
How do you build driveways and walkways for Edmonton winters?
Edmonton's freeze-thaw cycle is hard on flatwork, so the base preparation matters as much as the surface. We build driveways, paths and steps on properly compacted, well-drained bases and detail them to manage frost movement, which is what keeps concrete and pavers from heaving and cracking through the seasons. Because we did the grading too, the surface water has somewhere to go.
Will the planting survive the first Edmonton winter?
We select sod, trees and shrubs suited to our local climate and hardiness zone, and we time and prepare the planting to give it the best chance of establishing. Hardy, climate-appropriate species — placed with the right soil and drainage — are far more likely to come through that first winter than generic choices, which is why the plant selection is part of the plan rather than an afterthought.
